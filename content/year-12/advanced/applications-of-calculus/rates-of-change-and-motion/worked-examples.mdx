# Rates of Change and Motion

## Example 1 — Displacement, velocity and acceleration

**Problem:** A particle moves along a line. Its displacement at time $t$ seconds is $x(t) = t^3 - 9t^2 + 24t - 5$ metres.

**(a)** Find its velocity and acceleration at $t = 2$.
**(b)** Find when the particle is at rest.
**(c)** Determine whether the particle is accelerating or decelerating at $t = 2$.

**Part (a) — Differentiate.**

$$v(t) = x'(t) = 3t^2 - 18t + 24$$
$$a(t) = v'(t) = 6t - 18$$

At $t = 2$:
$$v(2) = 3(4) - 18(2) + 24 = 12 - 36 + 24 = 0 \text{ m/s}$$
$$a(2) = 6(2) - 18 = -6 \text{ m/s}^2$$

**Part (b) — Solve $v(t) = 0$.**

$$3t^2 - 18t + 24 = 3(t^2 - 6t + 8) = 3(t-2)(t-4) = 0$$

Particle is at rest at $t = 2$ s and $t = 4$ s.

**Part (c) — Interpret.**

At $t = 2$: $v(2) = 0$ and $a(2) = -6 < 0$. The particle is momentarily at rest and about to move in the negative direction.

## Example 2 — Recovering displacement from velocity

**Problem:** A particle starts at position $x = -2$ m. Its velocity at time $t$ seconds is $v(t) = 6t^2 - 4t + 1$ m/s.

Find its displacement at $t = 3$.

**Step 1 — Integrate velocity.**

$$x(t) = \int (6t^2 - 4t + 1)\,dt = 2t^3 - 2t^2 + t + C$$

**Step 2 — Apply the initial condition.**

$x(0) = -2$: $\quad C = -2$

$$x(t) = 2t^3 - 2t^2 + t - 2$$

**Step 3 — Evaluate at $t = 3$.**

$$x(3) = 2(27) - 2(9) + 3 - 2 = 54 - 18 + 3 - 2 = 37 \text{ m}$$

## Example 3 — Total distance vs displacement

**Problem:** A particle has velocity $v(t) = t^2 - 4$ m/s for $0 \leq t \leq 4$. Find the total distance travelled.

**Step 1 — Find when the particle reverses direction.**

$v(t) = 0$: $t^2 = 4 \Rightarrow t = 2$ (taking positive $t$).

- For $0 < t < 2$: $v < 0$ (moving in negative direction)
- For $t > 2$: $v > 0$ (moving in positive direction)

**Step 2 — Calculate distance in each phase.**

$$\text{Distance, } 0 \text{ to } 2 = \left|\int_0^2 (t^2-4)\,dt\right| = \left|\left[\frac{t^3}{3}-4t\right]_0^2\right| = \left|\frac{8}{3}-8\right| = \frac{16}{3} \text{ m}$$

$$\text{Distance, } 2 \text{ to } 4 = \int_2^4 (t^2-4)\,dt = \left[\frac{t^3}{3}-4t\right]_2^4 = \left(\frac{64}{3}-16\right) - \left(\frac{8}{3}-8\right) = \frac{56}{3}-8 = \frac{32}{3} \text{ m}$$

**Step 3 — Total distance.**

$$\frac{16}{3} + \frac{32}{3} = \frac{48}{3} = 16 \text{ m}$$

(Compare with displacement $= \displaystyle\int_0^4 (t^2-4)\,dt = \dfrac{32}{3} - 16 = -\dfrac{16}{3}$ m — a very different value.)
