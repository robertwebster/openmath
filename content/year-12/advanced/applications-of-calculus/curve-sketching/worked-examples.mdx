# Curve Sketching

## Example 1 — Finding stationary points and their nature

**Problem:** For $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 - 9x + 5$, find all stationary points and determine their nature.

**Step 1 — Differentiate.**

$$f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x - 9 = 3(x^2 - 2x - 3) = 3(x - 3)(x + 1)$$

**Step 2 — Solve $f'(x) = 0$.**

$$3(x - 3)(x + 1) = 0 \implies x = 3 \text{ or } x = -1$$

**Step 3 — Classify using the second derivative test.**

$$f''(x) = 6x - 6$$

At $x = -1$: $f''(-1) = 6(-1) - 6 = -12 < 0$ → **local maximum**

At $x = 3$: $f''(3) = 6(3) - 6 = 12 > 0$ → **local minimum**

**Step 4 — Find the $y$-values.**

$$f(-1) = (-1)^3 - 3(-1)^2 - 9(-1) + 5 = -1 - 3 + 9 + 5 = 10$$

$$f(3) = 27 - 27 - 27 + 5 = -22$$

**Stationary points:** local maximum at $(-1, 10)$ and local minimum at $(3, -22)$.

## Example 2 — Identifying points of inflection

**Problem:** Find the point of inflection of $g(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 12x - 4$ and describe the concavity on either side.

**Step 1 — Find $g''(x)$.**

$$g'(x) = 3x^2 - 12x + 12$$
$$g''(x) = 6x - 12$$

**Step 2 — Solve $g''(x) = 0$.**

$$6x - 12 = 0 \implies x = 2$$

**Step 3 — Check the sign change.**

| $x$ | $g''(x)$ | Concavity |
|-----|----------|-----------|
| $x < 2$ | $< 0$ | Concave down |
| $x > 2$ | $> 0$ | Concave up |

Concavity changes at $x = 2$, so there is a **point of inflection**.

**Step 4 — Find the $y$-value.**

$$g(2) = 8 - 24 + 24 - 4 = 4$$

**Point of inflection at $(2, 4)$.**

## Example 3 — Curve analysis with an exponential function

**Problem:** For $f(x) = xe^{-x}$, find the stationary point and determine its nature.

**Step 1 — Differentiate using the product rule.**

Let $u = x$ and $v = e^{-x}$:

$$f'(x) = e^{-x} + x \cdot (-e^{-x}) = e^{-x}(1 - x)$$

**Step 2 — Solve $f'(x) = 0$.**

Since $e^{-x} > 0$ for all $x$:

$$1 - x = 0 \implies x = 1$$

**Step 3 — Apply the second derivative test.**

$$f''(x) = -e^{-x}(1 - x) + e^{-x}(-1) = e^{-x}(x - 2)$$

$$f''(1) = e^{-1}(1 - 2) = -\frac{1}{e} < 0 \implies \text{local maximum}$$

**Step 4 — Find the $y$-value.**

$$f(1) = 1 \cdot e^{-1} = \frac{1}{e} \approx 0.368$$

**Local maximum at $\left(1, \dfrac{1}{e}\right)$.**
