# Combining the differentiation rules

## Example 1 — Product rule

**Problem:** Find $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(x^2\ln x\right)$.

**The product rule:** if $y = u\,v$, then $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = u'v + uv'$.

Let $u = x^2$ and $v = \ln x$:

$$u' = 2x \qquad v' = \frac{1}{x}$$

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = 2x \cdot \ln x + x^2 \cdot \frac{1}{x} = 2x\ln x + x$$

This can be written as $x(2\ln x + 1)$.

---

## Example 2 — Quotient rule

**Problem:** Find $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac{x}{\cos x}\right)$.

**The quotient rule:** if $y = \dfrac{u}{v}$, then $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{u'v - uv'}{v^2}$.

Let $u = x$ and $v = \cos x$:

$$u' = 1 \qquad v' = -\sin x$$

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1 \cdot \cos x - x \cdot (-\sin x)}{\cos^2 x} = \frac{\cos x + x\sin x}{\cos^2 x}$$

---

## Example 3 — Chain rule inside a product

**Problem:** Find $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(e^{2x}\sin x\right)$.

This requires the product rule, but the first factor also needs the chain rule.

Let $u = e^{2x}$ and $v = \sin x$:

$$u' = 2e^{2x} \qquad v' = \cos x$$

Apply the product rule:

$$\frac{d}{dx}(e^{2x}\sin x) = 2e^{2x}\sin x + e^{2x}\cos x = e^{2x}(2\sin x + \cos x)$$

Factoring out $e^{2x}$ gives a cleaner form.
