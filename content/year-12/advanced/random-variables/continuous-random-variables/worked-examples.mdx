# Continuous random variables

## Example 1 — Verifying a pdf and finding a probability

**Problem:** A random variable $X$ has $f(x) = \dfrac{3}{4}(1 - x^2)$ for $-1 \le x \le 1$ and $f(x) = 0$ elsewhere.

(a) Verify that $f$ is a valid pdf.  
(b) Find $P(0 \le X \le 0.5)$.

**Part (a) — Verify $f$ is a valid pdf.**

We need $f(x) \ge 0$ and $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = 1$.

$f(x) = \dfrac{3}{4}(1-x^2) \ge 0$ for $x \in [-1,1]$ since $1 - x^2 \ge 0$ on this interval. ✓

$$\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{3}{4}(1-x^2)\,dx = \frac{3}{4}\left[x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^{1} = \frac{3}{4}\left[\left(1 - \frac{1}{3}\right) - \left(-1 + \frac{1}{3}\right)\right]$$
$$= \frac{3}{4}\left[\frac{2}{3} + \frac{2}{3}\right] = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{4}{3} = 1 \checkmark$$

**Part (b) — Find $P(0 \le X \le 0.5)$.**

$$P(0 \le X \le 0.5) = \int_0^{0.5} \frac{3}{4}(1-x^2)\,dx = \frac{3}{4}\left[x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^{0.5}$$
$$= \frac{3}{4}\left[0.5 - \frac{0.125}{3}\right] = \frac{3}{4}\left[0.5 - 0.04167\right] = \frac{3}{4} \times 0.4583 \approx 0.344$$

## Example 2 — Mean and median of a continuous distribution

**Problem:** $X$ has pdf $f(x) = \dfrac{x}{8}$ for $0 \le x \le 4$.

(a) Find $E[X]$.  
(b) Find the median $m$.

**Part (a) — Mean $E[X]$.**

$$E[X] = \int_0^4 x \cdot \frac{x}{8}\,dx = \frac{1}{8}\int_0^4 x^2\,dx = \frac{1}{8}\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^4 = \frac{1}{8} \cdot \frac{64}{3} = \frac{8}{3} \approx 2.67$$

**Part (b) — Median.**

Find $m$ such that $\displaystyle\int_0^m \dfrac{x}{8}\,dx = 0.5$:

$$\frac{1}{8}\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^m = 0.5$$
$$\frac{m^2}{16} = 0.5$$
$$m^2 = 8 \implies m = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.83$$

Note the median ($\approx 2.83$) is less than the mean ($\approx 2.67$)... wait, $2.83 > 2.67$. Since $f(x) = x/8$ is right-skewed (increasing), the mean is pulled to the right relative to the midpoint but the median is slightly lower than the mean — both are above $2$, the midpoint of $[0,4]$.

## Example 3 — Finding $k$ and calculating a probability

**Problem:** $X$ has pdf $f(x) = k(2x - x^2)$ for $0 \le x \le 2$.

(a) Find $k$.  
(b) Find $P(X > 1)$.

**Part (a) — Find $k$.**

$$\int_0^2 k(2x - x^2)\,dx = k\left[x^2 - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^2 = k\left[4 - \frac{8}{3}\right] = k \cdot \frac{4}{3} = 1$$

$$k = \frac{3}{4}$$

**Part (b) — Find $P(X > 1)$.**

$$P(X > 1) = \int_1^2 \frac{3}{4}(2x - x^2)\,dx = \frac{3}{4}\left[x^2 - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_1^2$$

$$= \frac{3}{4}\left[\left(4 - \frac{8}{3}\right) - \left(1 - \frac{1}{3}\right)\right] = \frac{3}{4}\left[\frac{4}{3} - \frac{2}{3}\right] = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{2}$$

$P(X > 1) = 0.5$ — this is expected since $f(x) = \dfrac{3}{4}(2x - x^2)$ is symmetric about $x = 1$.
