# Logarithmic scales

## Example 1 — Decibel calculations

**Problem:** A busy restaurant has a background noise intensity of $10^{-5}$ W/m². A quiet library has intensity $10^{-9}$ W/m². Calculate the loudness of each in decibels, and find the difference in loudness.

Use $L = 10\log_{10}\!\left(\dfrac{I}{I_0}\right)$ with $I_0 = 10^{-12}$ W/m².

**Step 1 — Restaurant loudness.**

$$\frac{I}{I_0} = \frac{10^{-5}}{10^{-12}} = 10^7$$
$$L_{\text{restaurant}} = 10\log_{10}(10^7) = 70 \text{ dB}$$

**Step 2 — Library loudness.**

$$\frac{I}{I_0} = \frac{10^{-9}}{10^{-12}} = 10^3$$
$$L_{\text{library}} = 10\log_{10}(10^3) = 30 \text{ dB}$$

**Step 3 — Difference in loudness.**

$$\Delta L = 70 - 30 = 40 \text{ dB}$$

Although $40$ dB sounds modest, the intensities differ by a factor of $10^4 = 10\,000$.

## Example 2 — Richter scale comparison

**Problem:** An earthquake of magnitude $5.0$ causes minor damage. How much stronger (in terms of wave amplitude) would a magnitude $7.5$ earthquake be? Round to 1 decimal place.

**Step 1 — Write the amplitude ratio.**

The Richter magnitude is $M = \log_{10}(A/A_0)$, so:
$$\frac{A_2}{A_1} = 10^{M_2 - M_1}$$

**Step 2 — Calculate.**

$$\frac{A_2}{A_1} = 10^{7.5 - 5.0} = 10^{2.5} = \sqrt{10^5} \approx 316.2$$

The magnitude-$7.5$ earthquake has wave amplitudes about $316$ times larger.

## Example 3 — pH and hydrogen ion concentration

**Problem:** A swimming pool manager measures pH $7.8$ (slightly alkaline). Adding a chemical lowers the pH to $6.8$. By what factor does the $[\text{H}^+]$ concentration increase? Round to 1 decimal place.

**Step 1 — Express concentrations.**

$$[\text{H}^+] = 10^{-\text{pH}}$$
$$[\text{H}^+]_{\text{before}} = 10^{-7.8}, \qquad [\text{H}^+]_{\text{after}} = 10^{-6.8}$$

**Step 2 — Find the ratio.**

$$\frac{[\text{H}^+]_{\text{after}}}{[\text{H}^+]_{\text{before}}} = \frac{10^{-6.8}}{10^{-7.8}} = 10^{-6.8 + 7.8} = 10^{1.0} = 10$$

A drop of $1$ pH unit always corresponds to a $10\times$ increase in hydrogen ion concentration.
