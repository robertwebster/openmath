# Modelling periodic phenomena

## Example 1 — Reading amplitude, period and midline from a model

**Problem:** A tide is modelled by $H(t) = 2.8\sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{6}\right) + 4.2$, where $H$ is height in metres and $t$ is hours after midnight. State the amplitude, period and midline. Find the maximum and minimum heights.

**Step 1 — Identify the parameters.**

The model has the form $H = a\sin(bt) + d$ with $a = 2.8$, $b = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $d = 4.2$.

**Step 2 — Extract each feature.**

| Feature | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Amplitude | $|a|$ | $2.8$ m |
| Period | $\dfrac{2\pi}{b} = \dfrac{2\pi}{\pi/6}$ | $12$ hours |
| Midline | $H = d$ | $H = 4.2$ m |

**Step 3 — Find maximum and minimum.**

$$H_{\max} = d + a = 4.2 + 2.8 = 7.0 \text{ m}$$
$$H_{\min} = d - a = 4.2 - 2.8 = 1.4 \text{ m}$$

## Example 2 — Fitting a model to data

**Problem:** The daily maximum temperature (°C) in a coastal town is recorded each month. The highest average is $32$ °C in January (month $1$) and the lowest is $10$ °C in July (month $7$). Fit a cosine model of the form $T(m) = a\cos(b(m - c)) + d$.

**Step 1 — Find amplitude $a$ and midline $d$.**

$$a = \frac{32 - 10}{2} = 11, \qquad d = \frac{32 + 10}{2} = 21$$

**Step 2 — Find $b$ from the period.**

The temperature completes one full annual cycle over $12$ months:
$$b = \frac{2\pi}{12} = \frac{\pi}{6}$$

**Step 3 — Find phase shift $c$.**

Cosine achieves its maximum when $b(m - c) = 0$, so $m = c$. The maximum occurs in January ($m = 1$):
$$c = 1$$

**The model:**

$$T(m) = 11\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{6}\right) + 21$$

Check at $m = 7$: $T(7) = 11\cos(\pi) + 21 = -11 + 21 = 10$ °C ✓

## Example 3 — Using a model to solve for time

**Problem:** Using the tidal model $H(t) = 3\sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{6}\right) + 4$, find all times in the first $24$ hours when $H = 6.5$ m.

**Step 1 — Set up the equation.**

$$3\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{6}\right) + 4 = 6.5$$
$$\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{6}\right) = \frac{2.5}{3} = \frac{5}{6}$$

**Step 2 — Find the reference angle.**

Let $u = \dfrac{\pi t}{6}$, so $\sin u = \dfrac{5}{6}$.

$$u_{\text{ref}} = \arcsin\!\left(\frac{5}{6}\right) \approx 0.9853 \text{ rad}$$

**Step 3 — Write all solutions for $u$ in $[0, 4\pi]$.**

Sine is positive in the 1st and 2nd quadrants, and the domain $t \in [0, 24]$ means $u \in [0, 4\pi]$, so there are two periods to consider:

$$u = 0.9853,\quad \pi - 0.9853 = 2.1563,\quad 0.9853 + 2\pi = 7.2685,\quad 2.1563 + 2\pi = 8.4395$$

**Step 4 — Convert to $t = \dfrac{6u}{\pi}$.**

$$t \approx \frac{6 \times 0.9853}{\pi} \approx 1.88 \text{ h}, \qquad t \approx \frac{6 \times 2.1563}{\pi} \approx 4.12 \text{ h}$$
$$t \approx \frac{6 \times 7.2685}{\pi} \approx 13.88 \text{ h}, \qquad t \approx \frac{6 \times 8.4395}{\pi} \approx 16.12 \text{ h}$$

The tide reaches $6.5$ m at approximately $t \approx 1.88$, $4.12$, $13.88$ and $16.12$ hours after midnight.
