# Loans and investments

## Example 1 — Building a recurrence table

**Problem:** A loan of $15,000 is charged at 1% per month. Monthly repayments are $600. Build a repayment table for the first 3 months and find the outstanding balance after 3 months.

**The recurrence:** $A_{n+1} = 1.01\,A_n - 600$, $A_0 = 15{,}000$.

| Month $n$ | Opening balance | Interest (1%) | Repayment | Closing balance $A_n$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $15,000.00 | $150.00 | $600.00 | $14,550.00 |
| 2 | $14,550.00 | $145.50 | $600.00 | $14,095.50 |
| 3 | $14,095.50 | $140.96 | $600.00 | $13,636.46 |

The outstanding balance after 3 months is **$13,636.46**.

Notice that each month a slightly larger proportion of the repayment reduces the principal, because the interest charge falls as the balance falls. This is the hallmark of a reducing-balance loan.

## Example 2 — Finding the repayment and total interest

**Problem:** A borrower takes out a $25,000 car loan at 6% per annum (monthly compounding) to be repaid over 4 years. Find:
(a) the monthly repayment, and
(b) the total interest paid.

**Step 1 — Identify parameters.**

Monthly rate: $r = \frac{0.06}{12} = 0.005$, $n = 48$ months, $P = 25{,}000$.

**Step 2 — Apply the repayment formula.**

$$M = P\,\frac{r(1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n - 1} = 25000 \times \frac{0.005 \times (1.005)^{48}}{(1.005)^{48} - 1}$$

$(1.005)^{48} = 1.27049$

$$M = 25000 \times \frac{0.005 \times 1.27049}{0.27049} = 25000 \times \frac{0.006352}{0.27049} = 25000 \times 0.023476 = \$586.90$$

**Step 3 — Total interest.**

$$\text{Total paid} = 586.90 \times 48 = \$28{,}171.20$$
$$\text{Total interest} = 28{,}171.20 - 25{,}000 = \$3171.20$$

## Example 3 — Deriving and using the balance formula

**Problem:** Derive the formula for the outstanding balance after $n$ repayments, then use it to find the balance after 2 years (24 months) on the loan from Example 2.

**Deriving the formula.**

Starting from $A_0 = P$ and $A_{k+1} = (1+r)A_k - M$:

$$A_1 = (1+r)P - M$$
$$A_2 = (1+r)^2P - M(1+r) - M$$
$$\vdots$$
$$A_n = (1+r)^n P - M\bigl[1 + (1+r) + \cdots + (1+r)^{n-1}\bigr]$$

The bracket is a geometric series:

$$\boxed{A_n = P(1+r)^n - M\cdot\frac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r}}$$

**Applying to the loan from Example 2** ($P = 25{,}000$, $r = 0.005$, $M = 586.90$, $n = 24$):

$(1.005)^{24} = 1.12716$

$$A_{24} = 25000 \times 1.12716 - 586.90 \times \frac{0.12716}{0.005}$$
$$= 28{,}179 - 586.90 \times 25.432$$
$$= 28{,}179 - 14{,}926 = \$13{,}253$$

After 2 years, roughly half the principal ($25,000) remains — reflecting the back-loading of interest in the early repayments.
