# Geometric sequences and series

## Example 1 — Finding the $n$th term and deciding if a limiting sum exists

**Problem:** A geometric sequence has first term $a = 12$ and common ratio $r = \frac{1}{2}$. Find $T_5$ and the limiting sum $S_\infty$.

**Step 1 — Find $T_5$.**

$$T_5 = ar^4 = 12 \times \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^4 = 12 \times \frac{1}{16} = \frac{3}{4}$$

**Step 2 — Check whether a limiting sum exists.**

Since $|r| = \frac{1}{2} < 1$, the series converges and $S_\infty$ exists.

**Step 3 — Calculate $S_\infty$.**

$$S_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r} = \frac{12}{1 - \frac{1}{2}} = \frac{12}{\frac{1}{2}} = 24$$

## Example 2 — Summing a finite geometric series

**Problem:** Find the sum of the first 8 terms of the series $5 + 10 + 20 + \ldots$

**Step 1 — Identify $a$ and $r$.**

$a = 5$, $r = \frac{10}{5} = 2$.

**Step 2 — Apply the sum formula.**

Since $r > 1$, use $S_n = \dfrac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}$:

$$S_8 = \frac{5(2^8 - 1)}{2 - 1} = 5(256 - 1) = 5 \times 255 = 1275$$

## Example 3 — Finding $a$ and $r$ from two terms

**Problem:** The 3rd term of a geometric sequence is 18 and the 6th term is $\frac{2}{3}$. Find $a$ and $r$.

**Step 1 — Set up equations using $T_n = ar^{n-1}$.**

$$T_3 = ar^2 = 18 \quad (1)$$
$$T_6 = ar^5 = \frac{2}{3} \quad (2)$$

**Step 2 — Divide to eliminate $a$.**

$$\frac{(2)}{(1)}: \quad r^3 = \frac{2/3}{18} = \frac{2}{54} = \frac{1}{27}$$
$$r = \frac{1}{3}$$

**Step 3 — Substitute back to find $a$.**

From $(1)$: $a \times \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^2 = 18 \implies \frac{a}{9} = 18 \implies a = 162$.

**Step 4 — Find the limiting sum.**

Since $|r| = \frac{1}{3} < 1$:
$$S_\infty = \frac{162}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} = \frac{162}{\frac{2}{3}} = 243$$
