# Applications of series

## Example 1 — Compound interest as a geometric sequence

**Problem:** $\$8000$ is invested at $4\%$ per annum compound interest. Find the value after 6 years (to the nearest cent), and find how many complete years it takes to exceed $\$10\,000$.

**Step 1 — Identify the geometric model.**

Each year, the balance is multiplied by $1.04$. So the balance after $n$ years is:
$$A_n = 8000 \times (1.04)^n$$

This is a geometric sequence with $a = 8000 \times 1.04$ and $r = 1.04$.

**Step 2 — Find the value after 6 years.**

$$A_6 = 8000 \times (1.04)^6 = 8000 \times 1.26532 = \$10\,122.55$$

**Step 3 — Find when the balance first exceeds $\$10\,000$.**

Solve $8000 \times (1.04)^n > 10000$:
$$(1.04)^n > 1.25$$
$$n \ln(1.04) > \ln(1.25)$$
$$n > \frac{\ln 1.25}{\ln 1.04} = \frac{0.22314}{0.03922} \approx 5.69$$

The investment first exceeds $\$10\,000$ after $n = 6$ complete years.

## Example 2 — Depreciation and recurrence relations

**Problem:** A machine is bought for $\$30\,000$ and loses $18\%$ of its value each year. Set up a recurrence relation for its value $V_n$ after $n$ years, and find after how many complete years its value drops below $\$10\,000$.

**Step 1 — Write the recurrence relation.**

$$V_0 = 30000, \qquad V_n = 0.82\,V_{n-1}$$

This gives $V_n = 30000 \times (0.82)^n$.

**Step 2 — Solve the inequality.**

$$30000 \times (0.82)^n < 10000$$
$$(0.82)^n < \frac{1}{3}$$
$$n \ln(0.82) < \ln\!\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)$$

Since $\ln(0.82) < 0$, dividing reverses the inequality:
$$n > \frac{-\ln 3}{\ln(0.82)} = \frac{-1.0986}{-0.19845} \approx 5.54$$

The value first drops below $\$10\,000$ after $n = 6$ complete years.

## Example 3 — Total salary as a geometric series

**Problem:** A graduate starts on a salary of $\$55\,000$ and receives a $3.5\%$ raise each year. Find the total salary paid over the first 6 years, to the nearest dollar.

**Step 1 — Identify the geometric series.**

Year 1: $\$55\,000$; Year 2: $\$55\,000 \times 1.035$; ...; Year 6: $\$55\,000 \times 1.035^5$.

This is a GP with $a = 55000$, $r = 1.035$, $n = 6$.

**Step 2 — Calculate the sum.**

$$S_6 = \frac{55000(1.035^6 - 1)}{1.035 - 1}$$

Compute $1.035^6 \approx 1.22926$:

$$S_6 = \frac{55000 \times 0.22926}{0.035} = \frac{12609.3}{0.035} \approx \$360\,266$$
