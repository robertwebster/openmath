# Area of a triangle — worked examples

## Example 1: Finding area given two sides and an included angle

In triangle $ABC$, $AB = 14$ cm, $AC = 10$ cm and $\angle BAC = 62°$. Find the area.

<WorkedExample>
  <Step>Identify the two known sides and the included angle (the angle between them).</Step>
  <Step>Here the included angle is $\angle BAC = 62°$, between sides $AB = 14$ cm and $AC = 10$ cm.</Step>
  <Step>Apply the formula $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times AB \times AC \times \sin(\angle BAC)$.</Step>
  <Step>$$A = \tfrac{1}{2}(14)(10)\sin 62°$$</Step>
  <Step>Evaluate: $\sin 62° \approx 0.8829$.</Step>
  <Step>$$A = 70 \times 0.8829 \approx 61.8 \text{ cm}^2$$</Step>
</WorkedExample>

## Example 2: Finding the included angle given area and two sides

The area of triangle $PQR$ is 56 cm², $PQ = 16$ cm and $PR = 11$ cm. Find the acute angle $\angle QPR$.

<WorkedExample>
  <Step>Write the area formula with the unknown angle.</Step>
  <Step>$$56 = \tfrac{1}{2}(16)(11)\sin(\angle QPR)$$</Step>
  <Step>Simplify the right side.</Step>
  <Step>$$56 = 88\sin(\angle QPR)$$</Step>
  <Step>Solve for $\sin(\angle QPR)$.</Step>
  <Step>$$\sin(\angle QPR) = \frac{56}{88} = \frac{7}{11} \approx 0.6364$$</Step>
  <Step>Find the angle.</Step>
  <Step>$$\angle QPR = \sin^{-1}(0.6364) \approx 39.5°$$</Step>
</WorkedExample>

## Example 3: Multi-step — cosine rule then area formula

A triangular paddock has sides 30 m, 40 m and 50 m. Find the area.

<WorkedExample>
  <Step>We need an included angle before we can use the area formula. Use the cosine rule to find the angle between the 30 m and 40 m sides (call it $C$, opposite the 50 m side).</Step>
  <Step>$$\cos C = \frac{30^2 + 40^2 - 50^2}{2 \times 30 \times 40} = \frac{900 + 1600 - 2500}{2400} = \frac{0}{2400} = 0$$</Step>
  <Step>$$C = \cos^{-1}(0) = 90°$$</Step>
  <Step>This is actually a right-angled triangle (a 3-4-5 Pythagorean triple scaled by 10). The area formula confirms:</Step>
  <Step>$$A = \tfrac{1}{2}(30)(40)\sin 90° = 600 \times 1 = 600 \text{ m}^2$$</Step>
  <Step>The area can also be checked using the standard $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \tfrac{1}{2}(30)(40) = 600$ m².</Step>
</WorkedExample>
