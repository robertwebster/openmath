# Network concepts — worked examples

## Example 1: Vertices, edges and degree

A network has vertices A, B, C, D and E with the following edges: AB, AC, BC, BD and CE.

Find (a) the total number of edges, (b) the degree of vertex B, and (c) verify using the handshaking lemma.

<WorkedExample>
  <Step>**(a) Count the edges.** List all edges: AB, AC, BC, BD, CE — that is **5 edges**.</Step>
  <Step>**(b) Degree of B.** An edge touches B if B is one of its endpoints. Edges involving B: AB, BC, BD — that is **3 edges**, so degree of B = 3.</Step>
  <Step>**(c) Handshaking lemma check.** The sum of all degrees must equal $2 \times$ number of edges.
  Degrees: A = 2 (AB, AC), B = 3 (AB, BC, BD), C = 3 (AC, BC, CE), D = 1 (BD), E = 1 (CE).
  $$\text{Sum} = 2 + 3 + 3 + 1 + 1 = 10 = 2 \times 5 \checkmark$$</Step>
</WorkedExample>

## Example 2: Directed flow network — source, sink and capacity

A directed network models water flow through a treatment plant. The diagram has:
- Source S, intermediate nodes A and B, sink T
- Directed edges: S→A (capacity 8), S→B (capacity 5), A→T (capacity 6), A→B (capacity 3), B→T (capacity 7)

Identify (a) the source and sink, (b) the total capacity leaving S, and (c) the total capacity entering T.

<WorkedExample>
  <Step>**(a) Identify source and sink.** The source is the vertex with only outgoing edges: **S**. The sink is the vertex with only incoming edges: **T**.</Step>
  <Step>**(b) Total capacity leaving S.** Edges leaving S: S→A (8) and S→B (5).
  $$\text{Capacity out of S} = 8 + 5 = 13 \text{ units}$$</Step>
  <Step>**(c) Total capacity entering T.** Edges entering T: A→T (6) and B→T (7).
  $$\text{Capacity into T} = 6 + 7 = 13 \text{ units}$$</Step>
  <Step>In this particular network, total capacity out of S equals total capacity into T (both are 13). This is a coincidence of the edge weights — the actual maximum flow may still be less, depending on intermediate constraints.</Step>
</WorkedExample>

## Example 3: Interpreting a network in context

A road network has five intersections (vertices) A, B, C, D and E. Roads are one-way (directed). The capacities represent vehicles per minute. The network has: A→B (12), A→C (8), B→D (10), C→D (5), C→E (6), D→E (9).

A is the entry point; E is the exit. Identify the source, the sink, and determine the maximum possible flow that could leave A.

<WorkedExample>
  <Step>**Source:** A — all its edges point away from it. **Sink:** E — all edges point into it (D→E and C→E).</Step>
  <Step>**Maximum possible flow leaving A:** Add the capacities of all edges leaving A.
  $$12 + 8 = 20 \text{ vehicles per minute}$$</Step>
  <Step>This is an **upper bound** on the flow. The actual maximum flow through the network (from A to E) may be less — it depends on which intermediate edges create a bottleneck. This is determined by the **maximum-flow minimum-cut theorem**, studied in the next sub-topic.</Step>
</WorkedExample>
