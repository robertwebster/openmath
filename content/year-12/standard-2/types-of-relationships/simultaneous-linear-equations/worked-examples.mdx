# Simultaneous linear equations — worked examples

## Example 1: Solving algebraically (elimination)

Solve the simultaneous equations $3x + 2y = 13$ and $x - 2y = 3$. Find the values of $x$ and $y$.

<WorkedExample>
  <Step>Look for a variable to eliminate. Both equations have $2y$ (with opposite signs), so adding them eliminates $y$.</Step>
  <Step>Add the two equations:</Step>
  <Step>$$(3x + 2y) + (x - 2y) = 13 + 3$$</Step>
  <Step>$$4x = 16 \implies x = 4$$</Step>
  <Step>Substitute $x = 4$ into the simpler equation $x - 2y = 3$:</Step>
  <Step>$$4 - 2y = 3 \implies 2y = 1 \implies y = 0.5$$</Step>
  <Step>Check: $3(4) + 2(0.5) = 12 + 1 = 13$ ✓ and $4 - 2(0.5) = 3$ ✓</Step>
  <Step>Solution: $x = 4$, $y = 0.5$. The lines intersect at the point $(4,\ 0.5)$.</Step>
</WorkedExample>

## Example 2: Break-even analysis

A food truck has weekly fixed costs of \\$300 and variable costs of \\$4 per meal. Each meal is sold for \\$9. How many meals must be sold each week to break even?

<WorkedExample>
  <Step>Write the revenue and cost equations in terms of $n$, the number of meals sold.</Step>
  <Step>$$\text{Revenue: } R = 9n$$</Step>
  <Step>$$\text{Total cost: } C = 4n + 300$$</Step>
  <Step>The break-even point is where revenue equals cost: set $R = C$.</Step>
  <Step>$$9n = 4n + 300$$</Step>
  <Step>$$5n = 300$$</Step>
  <Step>$$n = 60$$</Step>
  <Step>The food truck breaks even at 60 meals per week. Selling more than 60 meals generates a profit; fewer than 60 results in a loss.</Step>
</WorkedExample>

## Example 3: Comparing two options

Two internet providers offer the following deals. Provider A: \\$25 per month plus \\$0.50 per GB of data. Provider B: \\$10 per month plus \\$1.00 per GB. After how many GB do the plans cost the same, and which plan is cheaper for heavy data users?

<WorkedExample>
  <Step>Write a cost equation for each provider in terms of $g$ (gigabytes used per month).</Step>
  <Step>$$C_A = 0.50g + 25$$</Step>
  <Step>$$C_B = 1.00g + 10$$</Step>
  <Step>Set the two costs equal to find the break-even usage:</Step>
  <Step>$$0.50g + 25 = 1.00g + 10$$</Step>
  <Step>$$15 = 0.50g$$</Step>
  <Step>$$g = 30$$</Step>
  <Step>At 30 GB the plans cost the same (both cost \\$40). Below 30 GB, Provider B is cheaper. Above 30 GB, Provider A is cheaper — so Provider A is better for heavy users.</Step>
</WorkedExample>
