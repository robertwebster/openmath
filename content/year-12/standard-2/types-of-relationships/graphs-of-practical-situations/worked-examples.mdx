# Graphs of practical situations — Worked Examples

## Example 1: Quadratic model — finding a maximum

A ball is thrown upward. Its height $h$ (metres) at time $t$ (seconds) is:

$$h = -5t^2 + 20t$$

Find **(a)** the height at $t = 1$ s and **(b)** the maximum height.

**Part (a): Substituting $t = 1$**

$$h = -5(1)^2 + 20(1) = -5 + 20 = 15 \text{ m}$$

**Part (b): Maximum height**

For a quadratic $h = at^2 + bt + c$, the maximum (when $a < 0$) is at:

$$t = -\frac{b}{2a} = -\frac{20}{2 \times (-5)} = -\frac{20}{-10} = 2 \text{ s}$$

Substitute $t = 2$:

$$h = -5(2)^2 + 20(2) = -20 + 40 = 20 \text{ m}$$

The maximum height is **20 m**, reached at 2 seconds.

## Example 2: Exponential model — growth and decay

A car purchased for \$30 000 depreciates at 15% per year. Its value after $t$ years is:

$$V = 30\,000 \times (0.85)^t$$

**(a)** Find the value after 4 years.

**(b)** After how many years is the car worth less than \$10 000?

**Part (a):**

$$V = 30\,000 \times (0.85)^4 = 30\,000 \times 0.52200625 \approx \$15\,660.19$$

**Part (b):**

Try successive values of $t$ (or use trial and error):

| $t$ | $(0.85)^t$ | $V$ |
|-----|------------|-----|
| 6 | 0.3771 | \$11 313 |
| 7 | 0.3206 | \$9 617 |

The value first drops below \$10 000 in year **7**.

## Example 3: Reciprocal model

The time $t$ (hours) to complete a job varies inversely with the number of workers $n$:

$$t = \frac{24}{n}$$

**(a)** How long does the job take with 6 workers?

**(b)** How many workers are needed to complete it in 3 hours?

**Part (a):**

$$t = \frac{24}{6} = 4 \text{ hours}$$

**Part (b):**

$$3 = \frac{24}{n} \implies n = \frac{24}{3} = 8 \text{ workers}$$

**Key feature of reciprocal graphs:** as $n$ increases, $t$ decreases — but never reaches zero. As $n$ decreases toward zero, $t$ grows without bound. The graph has horizontal and vertical asymptotes at $t = 0$ and $n = 0$.
