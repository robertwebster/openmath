# Rates — Worked Examples

## Example 1: Converting compound units (speed)

Convert 108 km/h to metres per second.

**Method:**

To go from km/h to m/s, convert km → m (multiply by 1000) and h → s (divide by 3600).

$$108 \text{ km/h} = 108 \times \frac{1000}{3600} \text{ m/s} = 108 \div 3.6 = 30 \text{ m/s}$$

**Reverse:** to go from m/s to km/h, multiply by 3.6.

## Example 2: Fuel consumption

A car uses 52.5 litres of fuel to drive 700 km. Petrol costs \$2.05 per litre.

**(a)** Find the fuel consumption in L/100 km.

**(b)** Find the cost to drive 400 km.

**Part (a):**

$$\text{Consumption} = \frac{52.5}{700} \times 100 = 7.5 \text{ L/100 km}$$

**Part (b):**

Litres for 400 km:
$$\frac{7.5}{100} \times 400 = 30 \text{ L}$$

Cost:
$$30 \times \$2.05 = \$61.50$$

## Example 3: Energy rates in context

A gym session burns 420 kJ in 20 minutes, then the person rests and burns 80 kJ in 40 minutes.

**(a)** Find the burn rate during exercise in kJ/min.

**(b)** Find the burn rate at rest in kJ/min.

**(c)** Find the average burn rate over the full hour.

**Part (a):**

$$\text{Exercise rate} = \frac{420}{20} = 21 \text{ kJ/min}$$

**Part (b):**

$$\text{Rest rate} = \frac{80}{40} = 2 \text{ kJ/min}$$

**Part (c):**

Total energy = $420 + 80 = 500$ kJ. Total time = $20 + 40 = 60$ min.

$$\text{Average rate} = \frac{500}{60} = 8.3\overline{3} \approx 8.33 \text{ kJ/min}$$
