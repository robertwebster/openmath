# Ratios and scale — Worked Examples

## Example 1: Dividing in a ratio

Three business partners share a profit of \$42 000 in the ratio $2:5:7$.

How much does each partner receive?

**Method:**

Total parts: $2 + 5 + 7 = 14$

Value of 1 part: $\dfrac{\$42\,000}{14} = \$3000$

| Partner | Parts | Amount |
|---------|-------|--------|
| A | 2 | $2 \times \$3000 = \$6000$ |
| B | 5 | $5 \times \$3000 = \$15\,000$ |
| C | 7 | $7 \times \$3000 = \$21\,000$ |

**Check:** $6000 + 15\,000 + 21\,000 = \$42\,000$ ✓

## Example 2: Map scales

A map has a scale of $1:200\,000$.

**(a)** Two towns are 3.5 cm apart on the map. Find the actual distance in km.

**(b)** A road is 45 km long in real life. How long is it on the map, in cm?

**Part (a): Map → Reality**

$$\text{Actual distance} = 3.5 \times 200\,000 = 700\,000 \text{ cm} = 7 \text{ km}$$

**Part (b): Reality → Map**

$$45 \text{ km} = 4\,500\,000 \text{ cm}$$

$$\text{Map length} = \frac{4\,500\,000}{200\,000} = 22.5 \text{ cm}$$

## Example 3: Similar figures — length, area and volume

Two similar prisms have corresponding lengths of 3 cm and 6 cm (scale factor $k = 2$).

The smaller prism has: surface area $= 54$ cm², volume $= 27$ cm³.

Find the surface area and volume of the larger prism.

**Key ratios:**

| Measure | Scales by | Calculation |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| Length | $k = 2$ | $3 \times 2 = 6$ cm |
| Area | $k^2 = 4$ | $54 \times 4 = 216$ cm² |
| Volume | $k^3 = 8$ | $27 \times 8 = 216$ cm³ |

**Remember:** When two shapes are similar, you only need the scale factor $k$ to find everything. The length, area and volume ratios are always $k : k^2 : k^3$.
