# Proof techniques — Worked examples

This sub-topic builds the toolkit for constructing rigorous proofs beyond direct argument: proof by contradiction, existence and uniqueness proofs, and results about rational and irrational numbers.

---

## Background: rational and irrational numbers

A **rational number** can be written as $\dfrac{p}{q}$ where $p, q \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $q \neq 0$.

An **irrational number** cannot be written in this form — its decimal expansion is non-terminating and non-repeating.

Key facts used in proofs:
- The sum, difference, product and quotient (denominator $\neq 0$) of two rationals is rational.
- If $p^2$ is divisible by a prime $k$, then $p$ itself is divisible by $k$.

---

## Example 1 — Proof by contradiction: $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational

**Prove that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.**

**Step 1 — Assume the opposite.**

Suppose $\sqrt{2}$ is rational. Then $\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{p}{q}$ where $p, q \in \mathbb{Z}$, $q \neq 0$, and $\gcd(p, q) = 1$ (fraction in lowest terms).

**Step 2 — Derive consequences.**

$$2 = \frac{p^2}{q^2} \implies p^2 = 2q^2$$

So $2 \mid p^2$. Since squaring preserves parity, $2 \mid p$. Write $p = 2m$.

$$(2m)^2 = 2q^2 \implies 4m^2 = 2q^2 \implies q^2 = 2m^2$$

So $2 \mid q^2$, hence $2 \mid q$.

**Step 3 — Find the contradiction.**

Both $p$ and $q$ are even, so $\gcd(p, q) \geq 2$. This contradicts $\gcd(p, q) = 1$.

**Conclusion:** $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational. $\square$

---

## Example 2 — Irrationality of a logarithm

**Prove that $\log_2 5$ is irrational.**

**Assume the opposite:** $\log_2 5 = \dfrac{p}{q}$ for positive integers $p$ and $q$.

Rewrite in exponential form:
$$2^{p/q} = 5 \implies 2^p = 5^q$$

The left side is even (a power of 2); the right side is odd (a power of 5). An even number cannot equal an odd number — **contradiction**.

**Conclusion:** $\log_2 5$ is irrational. $\square$

---

## Example 3 — Sum of rational and irrational

**Prove that $5 + \sqrt{3}$ is irrational.**

This uses the general result: rational + irrational = irrational.

**Proof by contradiction:** Suppose $5 + \sqrt{3}$ is rational; call it $r$.

Then $\sqrt{3} = r - 5$.

The right-hand side is the difference of two rationals ($r$ and $5$), so it is rational.

But $\sqrt{3}$ is irrational — contradiction.

**Conclusion:** $5 + \sqrt{3}$ is irrational. $\square$

> **General principle:** If you can isolate an irrational quantity and show it equals a rational expression, you have a contradiction. Look for opportunities to rearrange.
