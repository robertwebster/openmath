# Velocity, Acceleration and Calculus — Worked Examples

## Example 1: Choosing the right form of acceleration

**Problem:** A particle moves along the $x$-axis with $v = 4x^3$ m/s. Find the acceleration when $x = 1$ m.

**Solution:**

Since velocity is given as a function of displacement, the form $a = v\dfrac{dv}{dx}$ is most direct.

Differentiate $v = 4x^3$:
$$\frac{dv}{dx} = 12x^2$$

Apply $a = v\,\dfrac{dv}{dx}$:
$$a = (4x^3)(12x^2) = 48x^5$$

At $x = 1$:
$$a = 48(1)^5 = 48 \text{ m/s}^2$$

**Key idea:** When $v$ is given as a function of $x$, use $a = v\dfrac{dv}{dx}$ or equivalently $a = \dfrac{d}{dx}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{2}v^2\right)$.

## Example 2: Finding velocity from acceleration using the energy form

**Problem:** A particle starts from rest at $x = 0$. Its acceleration is $a = 3 - x$ m/s² (for $x \geq 0$). Find the speed when $x = 2$ m.

**Solution:**

Use $\dfrac{d}{dx}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{2}v^2\right) = 3 - x$:

$$\frac{1}{2}v^2 = \int(3-x)\,dx = 3x - \frac{x^2}{2} + C$$

Apply the initial condition $v = 0$ at $x = 0$: $\;C = 0$.

$$v^2 = 6x - x^2$$

At $x = 2$:
$$v^2 = 12 - 4 = 8 \implies v = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.83 \text{ m/s}$$

## Example 3: Solving a separable ODE for velocity as a function of time

**Problem:** A particle moves with acceleration $a = 5 - v$ m/s². It starts from rest. Find $v$ as a function of $t$, and find the time to reach $v = 3$ m/s.

**Solution:**

Since $a$ depends on $v$, use $\dfrac{dv}{dt} = 5 - v$ and separate variables:

$$\frac{dv}{5-v} = dt$$

Integrate (with $v = 0$ at $t = 0$):
$$-\ln|5-v| = t + C$$
$$-\ln 5 = C \quad (\text{from }v=0)$$

So:
$$\ln\frac{5}{5-v} = t \implies v = 5(1 - e^{-t})$$

**When does $v = 3$?**

$$3 = 5(1-e^{-t}) \implies e^{-t} = \frac{2}{5} \implies t = \ln\frac{5}{2} \approx 0.92 \text{ s}$$

**Note:** The terminal velocity is $5$ m/s (when $a = 0$).
