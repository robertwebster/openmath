# Resisted Motion and Projectiles — Worked Examples

## Example 1: Horizontal motion with resistance proportional to velocity

**Problem:** A particle of mass $3$ kg moves horizontally. The only horizontal force is air resistance $R = 6v$ N (opposing motion). Given $v = 15$ m/s at $t = 0$, find (a) $v$ as a function of $t$, and (b) the distance travelled until the particle's speed halves.

**Solution:**

**(a) Velocity as a function of time**

Equation of motion:
$$m\dot{v} = -6v \implies 3\dot{v} = -6v \implies \dot{v} = -2v$$

Separate variables:
$$\frac{dv}{v} = -2\,dt \implies \ln v = -2t + C$$

Apply $v = 15$ at $t = 0$: $C = \ln 15$.

$$v = 15e^{-2t} \text{ m/s}$$

**(b) Distance until $v = 7.5$ m/s**

Use $a = v\dfrac{dv}{dx}$: the equation of motion becomes $v\dfrac{dv}{dx} = -2v$, so $\dfrac{dv}{dx} = -2$.

Integrate: $v = 15 - 2x$. Set $v = 7.5$:
$$7.5 = 15 - 2x \implies x = 3.75 \text{ m}$$

## Example 2: Falling body with resistance, finding terminal velocity and motion

**Problem:** A particle of mass $1$ kg falls from rest. Air resistance is $R = 0.2v$ N (taking downward as positive, $g = 10$ m/s²). Find the terminal velocity and show that $v = 50(1-e^{-0.2t})$.

**Solution:**

**Terminal velocity** (set $\dot{v} = 0$):
$$10 - 0.2v_T = 0 \implies v_T = 50 \text{ m/s}$$

**Equation of motion:**
$$\dot{v} = 10 - 0.2v$$

Separate variables:
$$\frac{dv}{10-0.2v} = dt$$
$$-5\ln|10-0.2v| = t + C$$

At $t=0$, $v=0$: $C = -5\ln 10$.

$$5\ln\frac{10}{10-0.2v} = t \implies \frac{10}{10-0.2v} = e^{t/5}$$

Solving for $v$:
$$10 - 0.2v = 10e^{-t/5} \implies v = 50(1-e^{-t/5}) = 50(1-e^{-0.2t}) \qquad \checkmark$$

## Example 3: Resistance proportional to $v^2$ — using the $v$–$x$ form

**Problem:** A particle of mass $2$ kg falls from rest with resistance $R = 0.08v^2$ N. Using $g = 10$ m/s², find the speed after falling $20$ m.

**Solution:**

Equation of motion (use $a = v\dfrac{dv}{dx}$, downward positive):
$$2v\,\frac{dv}{dx} = 2(10) - 0.08v^2 = 20 - 0.08v^2$$
$$v\,\frac{dv}{dx} = 10 - 0.04v^2 = 0.04(250 - v^2)$$

Separate variables:
$$\frac{v\,dv}{250-v^2} = 0.04\,dx$$

Integrate (noting $\int\dfrac{v\,dv}{250-v^2} = -\dfrac{1}{2}\ln|250-v^2|$):
$$-\frac{1}{2}\ln(250-v^2) = 0.04x + C$$

At $x=0$, $v=0$: $C = -\dfrac{1}{2}\ln 250$.

$$\ln\frac{250}{250-v^2} = 0.08x$$

At $x = 20$: $\dfrac{250}{250-v^2} = e^{1.6}$

$$250 - v^2 = 250e^{-1.6} \implies v^2 = 250(1-e^{-1.6}) \approx 250 \times 0.7981 \approx 199.5$$
$$v \approx 14.1 \text{ m/s}$$
