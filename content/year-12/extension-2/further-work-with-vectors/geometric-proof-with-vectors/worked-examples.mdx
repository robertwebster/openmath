# Geometric proof with vectors — Worked examples

Vector proofs work by expressing geometric objects (points, midpoints, diagonals) as position vectors or vector expressions, then using algebraic properties of the dot product, magnitude, and scalar multiplication to deduce geometric facts.

**Two key results:**
- $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ are **parallel** if $\mathbf{u} = \lambda\mathbf{v}$ for some scalar $\lambda$.
- $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ are **perpendicular** if $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 0$.

---

## Example 1 — Proving a quadrilateral is a parallelogram

**$ABCD$ is a quadrilateral. $P$, $Q$, $R$, $S$ are the midpoints of $AB$, $BC$, $CD$, $DA$ respectively. Prove $PQRS$ is a parallelogram.**

Write position vectors for the midpoints:
$$P = \frac{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}{2}, \quad Q = \frac{\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}}{2}, \quad R = \frac{\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{d}}{2}, \quad S = \frac{\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{a}}{2}$$

Compute the side vectors:
$$\overrightarrow{PQ} = Q-P = \frac{\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{a}}{2}, \qquad \overrightarrow{SR} = R-S = \frac{\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{a}}{2}$$

Since $\overrightarrow{PQ} = \overrightarrow{SR}$, sides $PQ$ and $SR$ are parallel and equal in length.

Therefore $PQRS$ is a parallelogram. $\square$

## Example 2 — Angle in a semicircle

**Circle with centre $O$ at the origin, radius $r$. Diameter $AB$ where $A = -r\mathbf{i}$, $B = r\mathbf{i}$. $P$ is any point on the circle with $|\mathbf{p}| = r$. Prove $\angle APB = 90°$.**

$$\overrightarrow{PA} = -r\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{p}, \qquad \overrightarrow{PB} = r\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{p}$$

$$\overrightarrow{PA}\cdot\overrightarrow{PB} = (-r\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{p})\cdot(r\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{p})$$

Using the difference of squares identity $(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v})\cdot(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}) = |\mathbf{u}|^2-|\mathbf{v}|^2$:
$$= -(r\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{p})\cdot(r\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{p}) = -(r^2-|\mathbf{p}|^2) = -(r^2-r^2) = 0$$

Since the dot product is zero, $\angle APB = 90°$. $\square$

## Example 3 — Medians of a triangle are concurrent

**Triangle $ABC$ has position vectors $\mathbf{a}$, $\mathbf{b}$, $\mathbf{c}$. Show all three medians meet at $G = \frac{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}}{3}$.**

The median from $A$ goes to $M = \frac{\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}}{2}$.

The point dividing $AM$ in ratio $2:1$ from $A$:
$$G = \mathbf{a} + \frac{2}{3}(M-\mathbf{a}) = \mathbf{a} + \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}-2\mathbf{a}}{2} = \frac{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}}{3}$$

By symmetry, the same calculation from $B$ (midpoint of $AC$) and from $C$ (midpoint of $AB$) also gives $\frac{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}}{3}$.

All three medians pass through $G$, so they are concurrent. $\square$
