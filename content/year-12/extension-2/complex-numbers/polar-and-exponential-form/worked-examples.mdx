# Polar and exponential form — Worked examples

Every complex number $z = a+bi$ can be written in polar form using its modulus and argument. This form makes multiplication, division and powers much easier to handle.

---

## Key definitions

The **modulus** of $z = a+bi$ is $r = |z| = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ (distance from origin).

The **argument** $\theta = \arg(z)$ is the angle from the positive real axis, measured anticlockwise.

| Form | Notation | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Modulus–argument | $r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)$ | Geometric intuition |
| Exponential (Euler) | $re^{i\theta}$ | Powers and products |

**Euler's formula:** $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta+i\sin\theta$ (taken as given at this level).

---

## Example 1 — Converting to polar form

**Write $z = -1+\sqrt{3}\,i$ in polar form.**

**Modulus:**
$$r = |z| = \sqrt{(-1)^2+(\sqrt{3})^2} = \sqrt{1+3} = 2$$

**Argument:** $z$ lies in the second quadrant ($\text{Re}(z)<0$, $\text{Im}(z)>0$).

Reference angle: $\arctan\!\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{1}\right) = 60°$

Since $z$ is in Q2: $\theta = 180°-60° = 120° = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$

$$z = 2\!\left(\cos\frac{2\pi}{3}+i\sin\frac{2\pi}{3}\right) = 2e^{i\cdot 2\pi/3}$$

---

## Example 2 — Multiplying and dividing in polar form

**Given $z_1 = 6e^{i\pi/4}$ and $z_2 = 2e^{i\pi/12}$, find $\dfrac{z_1}{z_2}$ in polar form and hence in Cartesian form.**

**Polar form of quotient:** divide moduli, subtract arguments.
$$\frac{z_1}{z_2} = \frac{6}{2}\,e^{i(\pi/4-\pi/12)} = 3e^{i(\pi/4-\pi/12)}$$

$$\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\pi}{12} = \frac{3\pi}{12}-\frac{\pi}{12} = \frac{2\pi}{12} = \frac{\pi}{6}$$

$$\frac{z_1}{z_2} = 3e^{i\pi/6} = 3\!\left(\cos 30°+i\sin 30°\right) = 3\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}i\right) = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}+\frac{3}{2}i$$

---

## Example 3 — De Moivre's theorem

**De Moivre's theorem:** For any integer $n$,
$$(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)^n = \cos(n\theta)+i\sin(n\theta)$$

This is a direct consequence of the exponential form: $(e^{i\theta})^n = e^{in\theta}$.

**Example: Compute $(1+i)^{10}$.**

Write $1+i$ in polar form:
$$|1+i| = \sqrt{2}, \quad \arg(1+i) = 45°$$
$$1+i = \sqrt{2}\,e^{i\pi/4}$$

Apply De Moivre:
$$(1+i)^{10} = (\sqrt{2})^{10}\,e^{i \cdot 10\pi/4} = 2^5\,e^{i5\pi/2}$$

$5\pi/2 = 2\pi+\pi/2$, so $e^{i5\pi/2} = e^{i\pi/2} = \cos 90°+i\sin 90° = i$.

$$(1+i)^{10} = 32i$$

> **Check by squaring progressively:** $(1+i)^2 = 2i$, $(1+i)^4 = -4$, $(1+i)^8 = 16$, $(1+i)^{10} = 16 \cdot (1+i)^2 = 16 \cdot 2i = 32i$ ✓
