# Differential equations — Worked examples

A **differential equation** involves a function and its derivative(s). The goal is to find a function $y = f(x)$ that satisfies the equation.

---

## Example 1 — Direct integration (simplest type)

**Solve $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 4x^3 - 2$ with $y(1) = 3$.**

**Step 1 — Integrate both sides with respect to $x$.**

$$y = \int (4x^3 - 2)\,dx = x^4 - 2x + C$$

**Step 2 — Apply the initial condition to find $C$.**

$$3 = 1^4 - 2(1) + C = -1 + C \implies C = 4$$

**Particular solution:** $y = x^4 - 2x + 4$

> **General vs particular:** the general solution $y = x^4 - 2x + C$ is a family of curves. The initial condition picks out the one curve that passes through $(1, 3)$.

---

## Example 2 — Separable equation

**Solve $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{y}{x}$ with $y(1) = 5$.**

**Step 1 — Separate variables** (move all $y$ terms to one side, all $x$ terms to the other).

$$\frac{dy}{y} = \frac{dx}{x}$$

**Step 2 — Integrate both sides.**

$$\int \frac{dy}{y} = \int \frac{dx}{x}$$

$$\ln|y| = \ln|x| + C_1$$

**Step 3 — Solve for $y$.**

$$|y| = e^{C_1}|x| \implies y = Ax \quad (A = \pm e^{C_1})$$

**Step 4 — Apply the initial condition.**

$$5 = A \cdot 1 \implies A = 5$$

**Particular solution:** $y = 5x$

---

## Example 3 — Mixture / flow problem

**A tank contains 50 L of pure water. Brine containing 3 g/L flows in at 2 L/min; the well-mixed solution drains at 2 L/min. Set up and solve the differential equation for the salt content $Q(t)$ (grams) at time $t$ minutes.**

**Step 1 — Rate in / rate out.**

$$\frac{dQ}{dt} = \underbrace{3 \times 2}_{\text{in}} - \underbrace{\frac{Q}{50} \times 2}_{\text{out}} = 6 - \frac{Q}{25}$$

**Step 2 — Separate variables.**

$$\frac{dQ}{6 - Q/25} = dt \implies \frac{25\,dQ}{150 - Q} = dt$$

**Step 3 — Integrate.**

$$-25\ln|150 - Q| = t + C_1 \implies 150 - Q = Ae^{-t/25}$$

**Step 4 — Initial condition:** $Q(0) = 0 \implies 150 = A$.

$$Q(t) = 150\left(1 - e^{-t/25}\right)$$

**Interpretation:** As $t \to \infty$, $Q \to 150$ g — the brine concentration (3 g/L × 50 L) is the long-run equilibrium, as expected.
