# Related rates and volumes of revolution — Worked examples

This sub-topic has two distinct techniques.

**Related rates:** two quantities are linked by an equation; differentiate implicitly with respect to time $t$ and use the chain rule to connect their rates.

**Volumes of revolution:** rotate a curve about an axis; the volume swept out is found by integrating the area of circular cross-sections.

---

## Example 1 — Related rates: expanding circle

**Water ripples outward in a circular pattern. The area of the circle is increasing at $12 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$. At what rate is the radius increasing when the radius is 4 m?**

**Step 1 — Write the linking equation.**

$$A = \pi r^2$$

**Step 2 — Differentiate both sides with respect to $t$.**

$$\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r\,\frac{dr}{dt}$$

**Step 3 — Substitute known values.**

$\dfrac{dA}{dt} = 12$, $r = 4$:

$$12 = 2\pi(4)\,\frac{dr}{dt} = 8\pi\,\frac{dr}{dt}$$

$$\frac{dr}{dt} = \frac{12}{8\pi} = \frac{3}{2\pi} \approx 0.477 \text{ m/s}$$

> **Chain rule:** The key step is $\dfrac{dA}{dt} = \dfrac{dA}{dr} \cdot \dfrac{dr}{dt}$. You always differentiate the geometric formula, then plug in the instantaneous values.

---

## Example 2 — Related rates: conical tank

**A conical tank (apex down) has height 8 m and base radius 4 m. Water flows in at $3 \text{ m}^3/\text{min}$. How fast is the water level rising when the depth is 2 m?**

**Step 1 — Express volume in terms of a single variable.**

By similar triangles: $\dfrac{r}{h} = \dfrac{4}{8} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $r = \dfrac{h}{2}$.

$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3}\pi\!\left(\frac{h}{2}\right)^{\!2} h = \frac{\pi h^3}{12}$$

**Step 2 — Differentiate with respect to $t$.**

$$\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{\pi h^2}{4}\,\frac{dh}{dt}$$

**Step 3 — Substitute $\dfrac{dV}{dt} = 3$ and $h = 2$.**

$$3 = \frac{\pi(4)}{4}\,\frac{dh}{dt} = \pi\,\frac{dh}{dt}$$

$$\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{3}{\pi} \approx 0.955 \text{ m/min}$$

> The volume formula must involve only **one** variable before you differentiate. Always use similar triangles (or the given geometry) to eliminate $r$ in terms of $h$.

---

## Example 3 — Volume of revolution: between two curves

**Find the volume of the solid formed by rotating the region between $y = x$ and $y = x^2$ (for $0 \le x \le 1$) about the $x$-axis.**

**Step 1 — Identify which curve is on top.**

On $[0, 1]$: $x \ge x^2$, so $y = x$ is the outer boundary.

**Step 2 — Apply the washer method.**

$$V = \pi\int_0^1 \!\left[x^2 - (x^2)^2\right] dx = \pi\int_0^1 (x^2 - x^4)\,dx$$

**Step 3 — Integrate.**

$$V = \pi\left[\frac{x^3}{3} - \frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^1 = \pi\!\left(\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{5}\right) = \pi \times \frac{2}{15} = \frac{2\pi}{15} \approx 0.419 \text{ units}^3$$

> **Washer method:** When rotating the region *between* two curves, subtract the inner $y^2$ from the outer $y^2$ before integrating. The formula is $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_a^b\!\left[y_{\text{outer}}^2 - y_{\text{inner}}^2\right]dx$.
