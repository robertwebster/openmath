# Sampling distribution of the mean — Worked examples

When we take repeated samples from a population, the **sample mean** $\bar{X}$ is not fixed — it varies from sample to sample. Understanding its distribution allows us to make probability statements about how close a sample mean is likely to be to the true population mean.

**Key results:**

If samples of size $n$ are drawn from a population with mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$, the sample mean $\bar{X}$ has:
- Mean: $\mu_{\bar{X}} = \mu$
- Standard error: $\text{SE}(\bar{X}) = \dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$
- Shape: approximately normal for large $n$ (by the Central Limit Theorem)

For a binomial $X \sim B(n, p)$ with large $n$, the normal approximation is:
$$X \approx N(np,\; np(1-p))$$

---

## Example 1 — Standard error of the sample mean

**Random samples of size $n = 100$ are taken from a population with $\mu = 60$ and $\sigma = 15$. Find the standard error of $\bar{X}$ and describe the distribution of $\bar{X}$.**

$$\text{SE}(\bar{X}) = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{15}{\sqrt{100}} = \frac{15}{10} = 1.5$$

Since $n = 100$ is large, by the Central Limit Theorem:

$$\bar{X} \approx N(60,\; 1.5^2) = N(60, 2.25)$$

The sample mean is approximately normally distributed with mean 60 and standard deviation 1.5.

---

## Example 2 — Normal approximation to the binomial

**$X \sim B(100, 0.4)$. Using the normal approximation, find $P(X < 35)$.**

First, check validity: $np = 40 \geq 5$ and $n(1-p) = 60 \geq 5$. ✓

Mean: $\mu = np = 100 \times 0.4 = 40$

Standard deviation: $\sigma = \sqrt{np(1-p)} = \sqrt{100 \times 0.4 \times 0.6} = \sqrt{24} \approx 4.90$

Standardise:

$$z = \frac{35 - 40}{4.90} \approx \frac{-5}{4.90} \approx -1.02$$

From a standard normal table, $P(Z < -1.02) \approx 0.154$.

So $P(X < 35) \approx 0.154$.

---

## Example 3 — Using the empirical rule

**$X \sim B(400, 0.5)$ is approximated by $N(200, 100)$ (SD = 10). Use the empirical rule to find $P(180 \leq X \leq 220)$.**

The interval $(180, 220) = (200 - 20,\; 200 + 20) = \mu \pm 2\sigma$.

By the **empirical (68–95–99.7) rule**: approximately 95% of values from a normal distribution lie within two standard deviations of the mean.

$$P(180 \leq X \leq 220) \approx 0.95$$

> **The empirical rule** is an efficient tool when $z$-scores are whole numbers (±1, ±2, ±3):
> - Within 1 SD of mean: ≈ 68%
> - Within 2 SD of mean: ≈ 95%
> - Within 3 SD of mean: ≈ 99.7%
