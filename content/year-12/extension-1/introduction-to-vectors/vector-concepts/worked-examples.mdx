# Vector concepts — Worked examples

A **vector** is a quantity with both magnitude and direction. Vectors are written in component form as $\mathbf{v} = a\mathbf{i} + b\mathbf{j}$ (or $(a, b)$), where $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$ are unit vectors along the positive $x$- and $y$-axes.

| Operation | Formula |
|---|---|
| Magnitude | $|\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ |
| Unit vector | $\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \dfrac{\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{v}|}$ |
| Addition | $(a, b) + (c, d) = (a+c, b+d)$ |
| Scalar multiple | $\lambda(a, b) = (\lambda a, \lambda b)$ |
| Direction angle | $\theta = \arctan\!\left(\dfrac{b}{a}\right)$ — check quadrant |

---

## Example 1 — Magnitude, unit vector, direction angle

**For $\mathbf{v} = (-4, 3)$, find (a) the magnitude, (b) the unit vector, and (c) the angle $\mathbf{v}$ makes with the positive $x$-axis.**

**(a) Magnitude:**

$$|\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{(-4)^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} = \sqrt{25} = 5$$

**(b) Unit vector:**

$$\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{1}{5}(-4, 3) = \left(-\frac{4}{5},\; \frac{3}{5}\right) = -0.8\mathbf{i} + 0.6\mathbf{j}$$

**(c) Direction angle:**

The vector is in the **second quadrant** ($x < 0$, $y > 0$). The reference angle is:
$$\alpha = \arctan\!\left(\frac{3}{4}\right) \approx 36.87°$$

$$\theta = 180° - 36.87° \approx 143.13°$$

> **Quadrant check:** always check the signs of $a$ and $b$ before using $\arctan$.

## Example 2 — Vector arithmetic

**Given $\mathbf{a} = 3\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j}$ and $\mathbf{b} = -\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$, find $2\mathbf{a} - 3\mathbf{b}$ and its magnitude.**

**Step 1 — Compute scalar multiples.**

$$2\mathbf{a} = 2(3, -1) = (6, -2)$$
$$3\mathbf{b} = 3(-1, 4) = (-3, 12)$$

**Step 2 — Subtract.**

$$2\mathbf{a} - 3\mathbf{b} = (6 - (-3),\; -2 - 12) = (9, -14)$$

**Step 3 — Find the magnitude.**

$$|2\mathbf{a} - 3\mathbf{b}| = \sqrt{9^2 + (-14)^2} = \sqrt{81 + 196} = \sqrt{277} \approx 16.64$$

## Example 3 — Magnitude–direction form to components

**A boat is travelling at 12 km/h on a bearing of 060° (measured clockwise from north). Express the velocity as a component vector in the form $a\mathbf{i} + b\mathbf{j}$, where $\mathbf{i}$ is east and $\mathbf{j}$ is north.**

Bearing 060° means 60° clockwise from north, which is 30° from the east axis (positive $x$-axis). But measured from the north (positive $y$-axis):

$$\text{East component (x):} \quad 12\sin 60° = 12 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 6\sqrt{3} \approx 10.39 \text{ km/h}$$

$$\text{North component (y):} \quad 12\cos 60° = 12 \times \frac{1}{2} = 6 \text{ km/h}$$

$$\mathbf{v} = 6\sqrt{3}\,\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j} \approx 10.39\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j} \text{ km/h}$$

> **Bearing vs angle:** A bearing of $B°$ (clockwise from north) gives east-component $|\mathbf{v}|\sin B°$ and north-component $|\mathbf{v}|\cos B°$. This is the opposite of standard angle conventions.
