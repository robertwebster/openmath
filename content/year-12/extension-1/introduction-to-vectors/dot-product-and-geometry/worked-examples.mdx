# Dot product and geometry — Worked examples

The **scalar (dot) product** of two vectors $\mathbf{a} = (a_1, a_2)$ and $\mathbf{b} = (b_1, b_2)$ is:

$$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2 = |\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\cos\theta$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between the vectors ($0° \leq \theta \leq 180°$).

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0$ | $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are **perpendicular** |
| $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \vert\mathbf{a}\vert\vert\mathbf{b}\vert$ | $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are **parallel** (same direction) |
| $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = -\vert\mathbf{a}\vert\vert\mathbf{b}\vert$ | $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are **anti-parallel** (opposite directions) |

**Projections:**

| | Formula |
|---|---|
| Scalar projection of $\mathbf{a}$ onto $\mathbf{b}$ | $\dfrac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{b}|}$ |
| Vector projection of $\mathbf{a}$ onto $\mathbf{b}$ | $\dfrac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{b}|^2}\,\mathbf{b}$ |

## Example 1 — Finding the angle between two vectors

**Find the angle between $\mathbf{u} = (1, 2)$ and $\mathbf{v} = (3, -1)$ to 2 decimal places.**

**Step 1 — Compute the dot product.**

$$\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = (1)(3) + (2)(-1) = 3 - 2 = 1$$

**Step 2 — Find the magnitudes.**

$$|\mathbf{u}| = \sqrt{1^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{5}, \qquad |\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{3^2 + (-1)^2} = \sqrt{10}$$

**Step 3 — Apply the formula.**

$$\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}\cdot\sqrt{10}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{50}} = \frac{1}{5\sqrt{2}}$$

$$\theta = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac{1}{5\sqrt{2}}\right) \approx \cos^{-1}(0.1414) \approx 81.87°$$

> **Always** ensure $-1 \leq \cos\theta \leq 1$ before taking $\cos^{-1}$. A value outside this range indicates an arithmetic error.

## Example 2 — Perpendicularity and finding an unknown

**Find the value of $k$ such that $\mathbf{a} = (2k, 3)$ and $\mathbf{b} = (1, -2k)$ are perpendicular.**

For perpendicular vectors, $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0$.

$$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = (2k)(1) + (3)(-2k) = 2k - 6k = -4k$$

Setting this equal to zero:

$$-4k = 0 \implies k = 0$$

Wait — that gives the trivial zero vectors. Let me choose a better setup: $\mathbf{a} = (k, 3)$ and $\mathbf{b} = (2, k - 4)$:

$$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 2k + 3(k - 4) = 2k + 3k - 12 = 5k - 12 = 0$$

$$k = \frac{12}{5}$$

**Check:** $\left(\tfrac{12}{5},\; 3\right) \cdot \left(2,\; \tfrac{12}{5}-4\right) = \left(\tfrac{12}{5},\; 3\right) \cdot \left(2,\; -\tfrac{8}{5}\right) = \tfrac{24}{5} - \tfrac{24}{5} = 0$ ✓

## Example 3 — Vector projection and geometric proof

**Part (a): Find the vector projection of $\mathbf{a} = (4, 3)$ onto $\mathbf{b} = (1, 0)$.**

$$\text{proj}_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{a} = \frac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{b}|^2}\,\mathbf{b} = \frac{(4)(1)+(3)(0)}{1^2+0^2}(1,0) = \frac{4}{1}(1,0) = (4,0)$$

This is just the horizontal component of $\mathbf{a}$ — the "shadow" of $\mathbf{a}$ along the $x$-axis.

**Part (b): Prove that the diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular.**

Let the rhombus have adjacent sides $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ with $|\mathbf{a}| = |\mathbf{b}| = \ell$ (equal side lengths).

The two diagonals are $\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$.

$$(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})\cdot(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}) = |\mathbf{a}|^2 - |\mathbf{b}|^2 = \ell^2 - \ell^2 = 0$$

Since the dot product is zero, the diagonals are perpendicular. $\square$
