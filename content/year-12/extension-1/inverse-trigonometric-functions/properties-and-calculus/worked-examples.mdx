# Properties and calculus

## Standard derivatives and integrals

The three inverse trig derivatives are:

$$\frac{d}{dx}\sin^{-1}(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\cos^{-1}(x) = \frac{-1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\tan^{-1}(x) = \frac{1}{1+x^2}$$

The standard integral forms (used with the chain rule in reverse):

$$\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}}\,dx = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{x}{a}\right) + C \qquad \int \frac{1}{a^2 + x^2}\,dx = \frac{1}{a}\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{x}{a}\right) + C$$

---

## Example 1 — Chain rule differentiation

**Problem:** Differentiate $y = \sin^{-1}(3x^2)$ and find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ at $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$.

**Step 1 — Apply the chain rule.**

Let $u = 3x^2$, so $\dfrac{du}{dx} = 6x$.

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - (3x^2)^2}} \cdot 6x = \frac{6x}{\sqrt{1 - 9x^4}}$$

**Step 2 — Evaluate at $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$.**

$$\frac{dy}{dx}\bigg|_{x=1/3} = \frac{6 \cdot \frac{1}{3}}{\sqrt{1 - 9 \cdot \frac{1}{81}}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{1}{9}}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\frac{8}{9}}} = \frac{2}{\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}} = \frac{2 \cdot 3}{2\sqrt{2}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$$

---

## Example 2 — Definite integral using the standard form

**Problem:** Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^3 \frac{1}{9 + x^2}\,dx$.

**Step 1 — Match to the standard form.**

Write $9 + x^2 = 3^2 + x^2$, so $a = 3$.

$$\int \frac{1}{a^2 + x^2}\,dx = \frac{1}{a}\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{x}{a}\right) + C = \frac{1}{3}\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{x}{3}\right) + C$$

**Step 2 — Apply the limits.**

$$\int_0^3 \frac{1}{9+x^2}\,dx = \left[\frac{1}{3}\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{x}{3}\right)\right]_0^3 = \frac{1}{3}\tan^{-1}(1) - \frac{1}{3}\tan^{-1}(0)$$

$$= \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{\pi}{4} - 0 = \frac{\pi}{12}$$

## Example 3 — Product rule with an inverse trig function

**Problem:** Differentiate $g(x) = x^2 \sin^{-1}(x)$ and evaluate $g'(0)$.

**Step 1 — Apply the product rule.**

$$g'(x) = 2x \cdot \sin^{-1}(x) + x^2 \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$$

**Step 2 — Evaluate at $x = 0$.**

$$g'(0) = 2(0)\sin^{-1}(0) + \frac{0^2}{\sqrt{1-0}} = 0 + 0 = 0$$

**A harder evaluation — find $g'(1/2)$.**

$$g'\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) + \frac{(1/2)^2}{\sqrt{1 - 1/4}}$$

$$= 1 \cdot \frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{1/4}{\sqrt{3/4}} = \frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{1/4}{\sqrt{3}/2} = \frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{6}$$
