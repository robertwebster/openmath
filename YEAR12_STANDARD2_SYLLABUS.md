# Year 12 NSW Mathematics Standard 2 — Syllabus Map

**Syllabus:** Mathematics Standard 11–12 (2024), Year 12 content taught from Term 4 2026
**Stage:** Stage 6 — Mathematics Standard 2
**Structure:** 9 focus areas across 5 areas of study (Algebra, Measurement, Financial Mathematics, Statistical Analysis, Networks) — practical, application-driven

> ⚠️ **Verification note:** Outcome codes (e.g. `MS-A4`, `MS-N3`) and focus-area boundaries should be confirmed against the official digital curriculum at [curriculum.nsw.edu.au](https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/learning-areas/mathematics/mathematics-standard-11-12-2024/content/year-12/). The working outcome `MAO-WM-01` (Working mathematically) applies across all focus areas. First HSC examination under this syllabus is 2027.

---

## Navigation and Quiz Structure

Content is organised into **two navigable tiers** — Topic (= NESA focus area) and Sub-topic. Strand / area-of-study is metadata only.

Three quiz levels exist, all drawing from a **single source of truth** at the sub-topic level:

| Level | What it covers | Use case |
|---|---|---|
| **Sub-topic quiz** | One specific concept | "I need to practise the cosine rule" |
| **Topic quiz** | All sub-topics in a focus area | "Test me on the whole of Annuities" |
| **Year 12 Standard 2 quiz** | All focus areas | "I have a trial HSC on everything" |

Topic and Year quizzes are aggregated automatically from sub-topic question banks.

## Year 12 Standard 2 Focus Areas

---

### Types of relationships
**NESA outcome:** MS-A4 (verify)
**Strand:** Algebra

| Sub-topic | Key content |
|---|---|
| Graphs of practical situations | Construct and interpret graphs of practical non-linear relationships (quadratic $y = ax^2$, exponential $y = ab^x$, reciprocal $y = k/x$); read and describe features from real-world graphs (max/min, rate of change, intercepts) |
| Simultaneous linear equations | Solve a pair of linear equations graphically and algebraically; identify the point of intersection as a break-even point; model and interpret practical problems (cost vs revenue, two payment plans) |

**Open Math notes:** Almost entirely application-driven — every question should sit in a real context (population, depreciation, pricing). Break-even questions are a reliable HSC favourite. Difficulty 3 questions should require setting up the equations from a word problem, not just solving given ones.

---

### Non-right-angled trigonometry
**NESA outcome:** MS-M6 (verify)
**Strand:** Measurement

| Sub-topic | Key content |
|---|---|
| Sine rule | $\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}$; find unknown sides and angles; the ambiguous case (two possible triangles) |
| Cosine rule | $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$; find an unknown side; rearrange to find an unknown angle $\cos C = \frac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab}$ |
| Area of a triangle | $A = \tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$; choose the correct rule for a given configuration |
| Applications and radial surveys | Bearings (three-figure and compass); radial (plane-table) surveys; multi-step problems combining sine rule, cosine rule and area |

**Open Math notes:** Diagrams are essential — flagged as needing image support, especially bearings and the ambiguous case. Difficulty 3 questions should be multi-triangle problems requiring a choice of rule and a bearing interpretation. Right-angled trig (SOHCAHTOA) is assumed from Stage 5 / Year 11 and can be lightly revised in difficulty 1 questions.

---

### Rates and ratios
**NESA outcome:** MS-M7 (verify)
**Strand:** Measurement

| Sub-topic | Key content |
|---|---|
| Rates | Convert between units and compound units; fuel consumption (L/100 km); heart rate; energy in kJ/Cal and food/exercise contexts; speed and average rates |
| Ratios and scale | Divide a quantity in a given ratio; scale drawings, maps and plans; ratio of lengths/areas/volumes for similar figures; use a scale factor |

**Open Math notes:** High everyday-relevance content; keep contexts authentic (recipes, medication dosage, building plans). Scale-factor questions linking length : area : volume ($k : k^2 : k^3$) are commonly misunderstood and worth a dedicated difficulty 2–3 set.

---

### Investments and loans
**NESA outcome:** MS-F4 (verify)
**Strand:** Financial Mathematics

| Sub-topic | Key content |
|---|---|
| Investments | Simple and compound interest; future value and present value; appreciation and inflation; shares, dividends and dividend yield |
| Reducing-balance loans | Loans where interest is charged on the reducing balance; use a spreadsheet/recurrence table to track repayments; compare loans; credit cards and interest-free periods |

**Open Math notes:** Compound interest and reducing-balance tables are the highest-frequency HSC topic in Standard 2. Most questions are numeric and check cleanly. Difficulty 3 questions should require building or reading several rows of a reducing-balance schedule, or comparing two financial products.

---

### Annuities
**NESA outcome:** MS-F5 (verify)
**Strand:** Financial Mathematics

| Sub-topic | Key content |
|---|---|
| Future value of annuities | Recognise an annuity as a recurring equal contribution; calculate future value using a recurrence relation or a future-value table; superannuation accumulation |
| Present value of annuities | Calculate present value; relate present and future value; model loan repayments and retirement drawdowns as annuities |

**Open Math notes:** Builds directly on Investments and loans — sequence it immediately after. Annuity-table questions require careful reading of a table value before the arithmetic; provide the table within the question stem. Difficulty 3 questions should combine an annuity with an earlier lump-sum investment.

---

### Bivariate data analysis
**NESA outcome:** MS-S4 (verify)
**Strand:** Statistical Analysis

| Sub-topic | Key content |
|---|---|
| Scatterplots and correlation | Construct and interpret scatterplots; describe direction, form and strength; estimate and interpret Pearson's correlation coefficient $r$; correlation vs causation |
| Line of best fit and regression | Fit a line by eye; least-squares regression line; interpret gradient and intercept in context; use the line for prediction (interpolation vs extrapolation and its risks) |

**Open Math notes:** Calculator/technology gives $r$ and the regression equation — questions should focus on interpretation, not hand-calculating regression. The correlation-is-not-causation point should be tested explicitly with a conceptual (multiple-choice) item. Extrapolation reliability is a classic difficulty 2 discussion converted to a numeric prediction.

---

### The normal distribution
**NESA outcome:** MS-S5 (verify)
**Strand:** Statistical Analysis

| Sub-topic | Key content |
|---|---|
| z-scores | Standardise a value $z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$; interpret a z-score; compare values from different distributions |
| The empirical rule | The 68–95–99.7% rule; proportions and probabilities within 1, 2, 3 standard deviations; percentiles and real-world quality/test-score problems |

**Open Math notes:** z-score and empirical-rule questions are reliably numeric and check cleanly. Comparing two standardised scores (e.g. two students in different subjects) is the signature application — build a dedicated difficulty 2 set. The empirical rule should be applied symmetrically; tail-proportion questions are a good difficulty 3.

---

### Network flow
**NESA outcome:** MS-N2 (verify)
**Strand:** Networks

| Sub-topic | Key content |
|---|---|
| Network concepts | Vertices, edges, weighted and directed networks; degree; connected networks; interpret a network diagram in context |
| Flow problems | Source and sink; capacity of edges; flow through a network; maximum-flow minimum-cut theorem; find the maximum flow and identify the minimum cut |

**Open Math notes:** Requires diagram support throughout. Minimal spanning trees and shortest path are assumed from the Year 11 *Networks, paths and trees* focus area and only lightly revised here. Max-flow/min-cut is the genuinely new content — difficulty 3 questions should ask students to justify the maximum flow by identifying the cut.

---

### Critical path analysis
**NESA outcome:** MS-N3 (verify)
**Strand:** Networks

| Sub-topic | Key content |
|---|---|
| Activity networks | Represent a project as an activity network from a precedence/activity table; immediate predecessors; dummy activities |
| Forward and backward scanning | Earliest start time (forward scan); latest start time (backward scan); float (slack) time |
| The critical path | Identify the critical path and minimum completion time; interpret the effect of delays |

**Open Math notes:** Diagram-heavy and procedural — well suited to a custom renderer that highlights the critical path. Float-time and EST/LST values are numeric and check cleanly. Difficulty 3 questions should ask for the effect on completion time of changing one activity's duration.

## Relationship to Standard 1 and Advanced

- **Standard 1** shares most Year 12 focus areas with Standard 2 but at reduced depth and without some topics (e.g. annuities, formal regression). Open Math targets **Standard 2** as the mainstream course; a Standard 1 view can later reuse the same sub-topic banks with a reduced selection.
- **Mathematics Advanced** treats functions, calculus and the normal distribution far more formally and is built as a separate track (see `YEAR12_ADVANCED_SYLLABUS.md`). Standard 2 and Advanced share *conceptual* overlap (normal distribution, bivariate data, trigonometry) but Standard 2 stays application-first and uses no calculus.
